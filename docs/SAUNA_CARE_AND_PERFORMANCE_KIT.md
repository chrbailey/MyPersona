# Sauna Care & Performance Kit

## Purpose and safety boundary

This is a deployable specification for a **traditional electric sauna**. It
combines inspection, temperature validation, wood care, and post-cleaning
sanitation without turning a wet, high-temperature room into a collection of
unlisted electrical appliances.

> **Do not mount a supplemental heater on the glass door, under the floor, or
> on the ceiling.** Glass doors are not structural heater mounts; an improvised
> floor or overhead heater creates burn, fire, shock, and glass-breakage risks.
> Do not use portable space heaters, heat lamps, heating mats, or extension
> cords in a sauna. Faster heat-up must come from correcting heat loss or from
> a sauna heater/control system listed for the room and installed according to
> its manual by a licensed electrician.

The authority having jurisdiction (AHJ), electrical code, heater listing, and
manufacturer instructions always override this guide. Stop using the sauna if
there is scorched wood, cracked glass, a loose heater, nuisance tripping, a
burning smell, exposed conductors, or a control that fails to stop heating.

## Kit architecture

Pack the kit in four labeled modules. Nothing in the cleaning module is stored
in the hot room.

### A. Inspect and measure

| Item | Minimum specification | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reference thermometer | Calibratable, remote-reading probe rated for the sauna's maximum air temperature | Acceptance measurement; display/logger stays outside if it is not heat-rated |
| Temperature sticks | Reversible liquid-crystal strips covering the expected range | Quick hot/cold-spot screening only; not a safety control |
| Probe stand/clips | Wood or other nonconductive material | Repeatable placement without touching metal, heater, glass, or stones |
| Hygrometer | Sauna-rated | Records humidity during a normal session |
| Inspection light and mirror | Battery device used only while sauna is cold and de-energized | Heater, bench underside, vent, and wiring inspection |
| GFCI/RCD tester | Correct for the local receptacle/protection system | Test only protected circuits as directed by the device manufacturer |
| Log cards/QR form | Room ID, date, ambient temperature, readings, faults, and sign-off | Traceability |

Temperature stickers drift in prolonged heat. Treat the probe as the reference,
replace damaged/discolored strips, and never use either device to bypass or
relocate the heater's high-limit sensor.

### B. Clean and recondition wood

| Item | Specification | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vacuum | HEPA-filtered with soft brush | Remove dry debris before adding moisture |
| Brushes | Soft nylon, white/non-marking pads, narrow bench brush | Dedicated to sauna use |
| Cleaner | Manufacturer-approved sauna/wood cleaner; otherwise a small amount of mild, unscented detergent in warm water | No chlorine bleach, ammonia, solvent, pressure washer, or strongly scented product |
| Bottles/cloths | Labeled spray bottle, two buckets, white microfiber cloths | Apply to cloth/brush rather than saturating wood |
| Abrasive | 120- then 180-grit paper and sanding block | Local restoration only, with dust extraction and respiratory protection |
| Protectant | Sauna-bench paraffin oil or product explicitly approved by the sauna maker | Benches only unless the maker permits other surfaces; never coat heater guards or stones |
| PPE | Nitrile gloves, eye protection, suitable dust mask/respirator | Follow each product's safety data sheet |

**Cedar and hemlock are cleaned the same gentle way, but refinishing is not
automatically interchangeable.** Cedar commonly tolerates an approved sauna
paraffin treatment; pale hemlock can blotch or darken. Test the exact cleaner,
sanding sequence, and finish on a hidden area. Preserve the factory finish and
warranty whenever its care manual is available. Never apply household varnish,
polyurethane, deck stain, linseed oil, or an unapproved combustible finish in
the hot room.

### C. Sanitize after cleaning

The preferred baseline is physical cleaning, complete drying, and normal sauna
heat. UV is an optional **unoccupied-cycle supplement**, not a substitute for
removing soil.

Use only a complete, listed germicidal UV-C appliance whose manufacturer
explicitly permits the room temperature, humidity, surface materials, and
intended fixed installation. The deployable UV module requires all of these:

1. Ozone-free lamp specification and no intentional ozone generator.
2. Keyed enable switch and cycle timer outside the sauna.
3. Door interlock that immediately removes lamp power when the door opens.
4. Occupancy/motion fail-safe independent of the door switch.
5. Status beacon outside and a durable “UV-C — DO NOT ENTER” label.
6. Lamp-break procedure, hour counter, and manufacturer replacement interval.
7. A commissioning radiometer/dosimeter suitable for the lamp wavelength.

UV-C can injure eyes and skin, degrades plastics and finishes, and only treats
surfaces reached by an adequate dose. Do not use a hand-held exposed lamp, do
not look at the source, and do not operate UV while a person or animal can enter.
The installer must validate that the safety interlocks fail safe; microbiology
claims and exposure time must come from the UV appliance manufacturer, not from
an assumed “minutes per room” rule. A closed, removable-item UV cabinet outside
the sauna is the safer alternative for small washable accessories.

Do not use ozone, fogging, or aerosolized disinfectant in the sauna. If a
contamination event requires a registered disinfectant, verify wood and heater
compatibility, contact time, rinsing, and ventilation on the product label and
sauna manual first.

### D. Service spares

Build the spares pouch from the exact model and serial number:

- manufacturer-specified heating element, contactor/relay, sensor/high-limit
  assembly, thermostat/controller, and fuses;
- correct heater stones (type and size specified by the heater manufacturer);
- approved door/interlock switch, lamp, and labels for an installed UV module;
- wiring diagram, installation/service manuals, purchase record, and lockout
  device.

Parts are inventory, **not user-service modules**. Do not substitute a higher
wattage element, bridge a thermal cutout, change conductor size, or replace a
controller with an unmatched unit. A licensed electrician or manufacturer-
authorized technician performs electrical diagnosis and replacement.

## Heating performance design

### Diagnose before increasing power

Record these inputs: interior length, width, and height; glass and other
uninsulated surface area; construction/insulation; heater make, model, rated
power and supply; stone condition; vent locations; setpoint; cold ambient
temperature; and time-temperature curve. Then check:

1. Door closes and seals without binding; glass and frame are intact.
2. Insulation and vapor barrier are continuous and dry where accessible.
3. Intake and exhaust match the heater/manual layout and are unobstructed.
4. Stones are the specified type, loosely stacked for airflow, not crumbled or
   packed around elements.
5. Each element is operating and supply voltage/current are checked by a
   qualified electrician.
6. Sensor and high-limit are in the exact manufacturer location and are not
   covered, moved, or spoofed.

A heater can be correctly sized only with the manufacturer's room-volume method,
including its adjustment for glass, masonry, and other uninsulated surfaces.
Do not infer heater power from volume alone.

### Approved upgrade path

Choose one of these, in order:

1. **Restore performance:** repair door sealing, insulation, ventilation,
   stones, failed elements, or controls using approved parts.
2. **Replace the primary heater:** select a listed sauna heater/control package
   whose permitted room range contains the manufacturer's adjusted room volume.
   Verify supply capacity, branch circuit, overcurrent protection, conductor
   temperature rating, ventilation, sensor position, guard, and every clearance.
3. **Engineered dual-heater system:** only when one manufacturer provides or
   explicitly approves the heaters, coordinated controller, sensors/high-limits,
   clearances, and room layout. Obtain AHJ approval and professional load/design
   calculations before purchase.

There is no generic door/floor/roof add-on heater in this design. A heater near
glass may require extra clearance or a protective wall specified by its manual;
ordinary glass is not that wall. Never add a second independent thermostat to
“push past” the primary limit. The target is the temperature range permitted by
the sauna and heater manufacturers—not “as hot as possible.”

## Deployment runbook

### 1. Survey and gate (day 0)

- Photograph the nameplates, controller, sensor, heater clearances, vents,
  panel/circuit identification, wood condition, glass, and door latch.
- Collect manuals and confirm local permit/AHJ requirements.
- Quarantine the room on any stop-use fault. Have the electrician establish a
  safe de-energization/lockout method.
- Approve chemicals, wood treatment, UV appliance, and electrical parts against
  the actual models before ordering.

### 2. Baseline test (cold start)

With no occupants, place the reference air probe at the manufacturer's normal
measurement location (typically seated head height, away from the heater,
door, vent jet, ceiling, and any surface). Put screening sticks at lower bench,
upper bench, and the opposite side at the same depth. Record ambient, then air
temperature every five minutes until stable or the normal setpoint is reached.
Do not exceed manufacturer limits and stop on odor, smoke, arcing, or abnormal
controller behavior.

Repeat the same placements after maintenance. Comparing identical cold-start
curves is more useful than chasing a single hottest spot. A qualified technician
investigates large asymmetry or cycling that does not match the manual.

### 3. Clean and restore (power isolated, room cold)

1. Remove accessories; vacuum ceiling-to-floor, including bench undersides and
   vents. Do not vacuum heater stones unless the heater manual directs it.
2. Damp-clean in wood-grain direction. Keep liquid away from controls, sensor,
   elements, lamp sockets, and wiring penetrations. Do not soak or hose wood.
3. Wipe with a separate barely damp cloth if the cleaner requires rinsing.
4. Ventilate until completely dry. Investigate persistent black growth, soft
   wood, or musty odor as a moisture/building problem rather than sanding it away.
5. For stains or raised grain, lightly sand dry wood with the grain, collect all
   dust, and feather edges. Do not thin structural slats or erase required labels.
6. Apply an approved bench protectant sparingly to clean, dry wood; wipe off all
   excess and observe the maker's cure time. Leave walls and ceiling untreated
   unless expressly approved.

### 4. Technical service and commissioning

The licensed installer services heater/control components and, if selected,
installs the fixed UV module or approved replacement heater. Commission in this
order:

- verify protective earth/bonding, circuit protection, terminations, guards,
  clearances, ventilation, sensors, and high-limit operation;
- prove UV timer, indicator, door interlock, and occupancy fail-safe before any
  germicidal cycle; validate delivered dose per the appliance protocol;
- perform a supervised empty-room heat cycle, then the repeat baseline test;
- run one empty-room burn-in/ventilation cycle required by the heater or wood
  product manufacturer before occupancy;
- attach signed electrical/UV commissioning sheets and update the equipment log.

### 5. Handover acceptance

Release the sauna only when all boxes are checked:

- [ ] No stop-use condition remains; permits/inspection are closed where required.
- [ ] Heater, control, sensor, guard, stones, vents, and clearances match manuals.
- [ ] Door opens freely outward and no added device impedes emergency exit.
- [ ] Repeat heat-up curve meets the documented manufacturer expectation.
- [ ] Reference probe and high-limit are not being used outside their intended role.
- [ ] Wood is dry, residue-free, splinter-free, and free of unapproved coatings.
- [ ] UV cannot energize with the door open or occupancy detected; labels and
      lamp-break procedure are present.
- [ ] Owner has manuals, circuit identity, service contacts, logs, and training.

## Maintenance schedule

| Interval | Owner/operator action |
| --- | --- |
| Every use | Sit on washable towels; remove wet items; wipe spills; leave room to dry; visually check heater and door |
| Weekly (or by use load) | Vacuum and gentle damp-clean high-touch wood; clean floor; check vents and unusual odors |
| Monthly | Inspect fasteners, splinters, glass, seals, heater guard and stones; compare a temperature spot check with the reference probe; test UV interlocks without exposing the lamp |
| Quarterly | Run and archive the standardized cold-start curve; inspect UV labels/hour count and material degradation; review fault log |
| 6–12 months | Professional electrical/heater inspection at the interval required by the manufacturer; deep clean; recondition approved benches only as needed |
| Manufacturer interval | Re-stack/replace stones, calibrate instruments, replace UV lamp, and service controls exactly as documented |

Increase frequency for commercial use. Never reset a recurring high-limit trip
without diagnosing its cause.

## Governing documents to collect

Use the current editions adopted in the installation location rather than
copying generic values into this plan:

- sauna, heater, controller, and UV appliance installation/service manuals;
- applicable electrical and building/fire codes and AHJ permit requirements;
- the heater's applicable certification/listing (for example, the sauna-heating
  equipment standard used by the local certification body);
- IEC 62471 or the locally adopted photobiological-safety framework for lamps;
- chemical labels and safety data sheets; and
- workplace UV exposure rules if employees operate or service the system.

This document intentionally omits universal wattage, clearance, wire-size,
temperature, and UV-dose numbers: those are product-, room-, and jurisdiction-
specific safety parameters that belong in the collected manuals and signed
commissioning record.
